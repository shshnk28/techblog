I work on search — specifically the part between someone typing a query
and getting back something they actually wanted.

This blog is where I think through that problem in public: hybrid
retrieval, query understanding, reranking, and where search and AI are
converging. Mostly posts I wish I'd found while figuring something out.

On Walmart's search runtime team I've worked on the recall and ranking
path of ecommerce search at scale — re-architecting the L1 recall stage
to merge lexical candidates from Solr with an ANN dense index into a
single set for a cross-encoder reranker, query fan-out and result-merge
in the search orchestrator, and a lot of unglamorous Solr tuning, which
is where much of the latency budget is won or lost. Before that, at
Tracxn, I ran large-scale indexing and query serving on Elasticsearch.

What pulls me toward relevance as a specialty is that it's equal parts
art and science. The science is measurable — judgments, offline metrics,
telling a real improvement from noise. The art is everything the metrics
miss: what a shopper actually meant, and how that shifts from person to
person once personalization enters.

Right now I'm going deeper on embeddings, vector databases, hybrid
retrieval and RAG — currently building a research-dossier system over
Indian listed-company annual reports.

I'm in Bengaluru, and open to Lead or Senior engineering roles in search,
relevance, or backend.
